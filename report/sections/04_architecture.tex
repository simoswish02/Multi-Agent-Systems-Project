\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The system is implemented from scratch in Python. This section walks through
its architecture bottom-up: the codebase layout and its configuration-driven
design (Section~\ref{subsec:arch-overview}), the environment implementation
(Section~\ref{subsec:arch-env}), the tensor encoding of the observations of
Section~\ref{subsec:prob-obs} (Section~\ref{subsec:arch-obs}), the neural
network that maps observations to action values
(Section~\ref{subsec:arch-network}), the context vector that conditions it
(Section~\ref{subsec:arch-context}), the learning machinery around it
(Section~\ref{subsec:arch-learning}), and the interactive tooling used for
inspection and evaluation (Section~\ref{subsec:arch-tooling}).

The codebase separates four concerns (Figure~\ref{fig:modules}): the
\emph{environment} (\texttt{env/}) implements the world model of
Section~\ref{sec:problem}; the \emph{agent} (\texttt{agents/}) contains the
neural network, the replay and $n$-step buffers, and the update rule; the
\emph{training package} (\texttt{training/}) owns the episode loop,
curriculum staging and domain-randomization sampling; and the \emph{GUI}
(\texttt{gui/}) renders episodes without ever influencing them. Entry points
(\texttt{main.py} for training and interactive modes,
\texttt{eval\_checkpoints.py} and \texttt{testing/evaluate\_policy.py} for
batch evaluation) are thin wrappers over these packages.

A single YAML file is the sole source of truth for every tunable quantity:
environment geometry and reward values, randomization ranges, curriculum
phases, network and optimizer hyperparameters, logging cadence. Nothing is
hard-coded twice, and one consequence is methodological: an experiment ---
including the full training curriculum --- is specified by editing
configuration, not code, which makes runs reproducible and diffable. The
same discipline governs derived quantities; for instance, the normalizers of
the context vector (Section~\ref{subsec:arch-context}) are computed from the
randomization ranges rather than stated independently, so widening a range
cannot silently desynchronize the network's inputs.

\subsection{Environment implementation}
\label{subsec:arch-env}

\texttt{DroneSearchEnv} realizes the model of Section~\ref{subsec:prob-model}
behind a Gymnasium-style interface \citep{towers2024gymnasium}, with one
essential extension: the native stepping primitive is
\texttt{step\_agent(i, action)} --- one call per drone per round, in fixed
round-robin order --- rather than a joint step. (A standard
\texttt{step(action)} wrapper that advances the round-robin pointer exists
for API compliance, but the training loop drives \texttt{step\_agent}
directly.) Episode setup follows a strict contract:
\texttt{set\_domain\_params(...)} first, then \texttt{reset()}. The former
installs the episode parameters $\theta = (\rvis, \rcomm, \nagents, \rho,
\pfault)$; the latter generates the world. Keeping the two separate matters
because the same parameters are also fed to the network as its context
vector: the contract guarantees that what the environment \emph{does} and
what the network is \emph{told} can never diverge.

\paragraph{World generation.}
\texttt{reset()} performs rejection sampling: it draws an obstacle map with
density $\rho$, forces the reference corner free, computes the
BFS-reachable region, and resamples (up to a bounded number of attempts) if
that region is trivial; the target is then drawn uniformly from the
reachable free cells. All randomness flows through a single seeded
generator, and map generation consumes it before the fault process does ---
so one seed reproduces the layout \emph{and} the fault sequence, a property
the evaluation protocol relies on
(Section~\ref{subsec:train-infra}).

\paragraph{Anatomy of a turn.}
A live drone's \texttt{step\_agent} call executes a fixed pipeline: (i)~the
fault coin flip of Section~\ref{subsec:prob-fault} (on a break, the rest of
the pipeline is skipped and the call returns a flagged no-op, the turn having
been spent all the same); (ii)~move validation and execution,
accumulating the reward terms of Table~\ref{tab:reward}; (iii)~vision update
of the drone's knowledge maps; (iv)~wreck-beacon detection;
(v)~communication fusion over the connectivity components of
Section~\ref{subsec:prob-comm}; (vi)~update of the team-level discovered
set, from which the exploration bonus is computed; (vii)~target check. A
wreck's call skips everything but the clock. The action mask of
Section~\ref{subsec:prob-model} is computed on demand per drone and
returned alongside the observation, so every consumer (training, evaluation,
GUI) applies exactly the same legality rule.

\subsection{Observation encoding}
\label{subsec:arch-obs}

The knowledge state $K_i^t$ reaches the network as two spatial tensors
(Table~\ref{tab:channels}), both full-map, normalized to $[0,1]$:

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{clll}
\toprule
\textbf{Ch.} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Stream} & \textbf{Encoding} \\
\midrule
0 & Visited        & both   & $\{0,1\}$ --- free cells discovered by drone $i$ (incl.\ fused) \\
1 & Obstacle       & both   & $\{0,1\}$ --- obstacles discovered by drone $i$ \\
2 & Trajectory     & both   & $\min(\tau_i(c)/5,\,1)$ --- own visit counts, saturating \\
3 & Target         & both   & $\{0,1\}$ --- target cell once seen (persistent memory) \\
4 & Own\_Position  & global & $\{0,1\}$ --- single $1$ at drone $i$'s cell \\
5 & Broken         & global & $\{0,1\}$ --- crash sites \emph{known to drone $i$} \\
4 & Other\_Position& local  & $\{0,1\}$ --- \emph{live} teammates currently within $V_i^t$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Observation channels. The \emph{global} stream
($6\times\gridsize\times\gridsize$) is consumed whole; the \emph{local}
stream ($5\times\gridsize\times\gridsize$) is cropped to a $13\times13$
patch centred on the drone before encoding. Channel 4 differs by stream: the
egocentric patch needs no own-position marker, but benefits from seeing
nearby teammates; wrecks never appear as teammates --- they surface in the
Broken channel instead, once known.}
\label{tab:channels}
\end{table}

Two encoding decisions deserve justification. First, \textbf{the drone's own
position is a spatial channel}, not an out-of-band coordinate pair: channel
4 of the global stream contains a single $1$ at the drone's cell. The
convolutional encoder can thus reason about position \emph{spatially} ---
its relation to obstacles, to the searched region, to known crash sites ---
rather than receiving it as two opaque numbers; and the network recovers the
integer coordinates exactly (by $\arg\max$ over that channel) whenever it
needs them, as the patch-cropping step below does. The position also travels
inside the observation through the replay buffer for free, instead of
requiring a parallel data path.

Second, \textbf{fault knowledge is spatial too}: the Broken channel marks
the crash sites drone $i$ knows about --- and only those
(Section~\ref{subsec:prob-fault}). Beyond the scalar belief $\hat n_i$, the
\emph{location} of a crash is informative: it tells the network \emph{which
region} lost its searcher, precisely the information needed to re-cover a
dead teammate's sector.

The local stream complements the global one at a different scale. Its
$13\times13$ crop --- sized to exceed the largest vision window
($2\cdot6+1 = 13$) --- retains full resolution around the drone, where
cell-level distinctions (adjacent obstacles, a teammate one cell away)
drive immediate action, while the global stream provides the coarse
strategic picture.

A single network (Figure~\ref{fig:network}) maps
$(o_i^{\mathrm{glob}}, o_i^{\mathrm{loc}}, \mathbf{c}_i)$ --- global stream,
local stream, context vector --- to the four action values. It fuses three
information pathways: a \emph{global encoder} for strategic structure, a
\emph{local encoder} for tactical detail, and the context for regime
awareness.

\paragraph{Global encoder.}
The global stream is processed by a ConvNeXt-style hierarchy
\citep{liu2022convnet}: three stages of inverted-bottleneck blocks
(depthwise $7\times7$ convolution, pointwise expansion, GELU, and Global
Response Normalization \citep{woo2023convnext}) with channel widths
$96 \to 192 \to 384$ at spatial resolutions $32 \to 16 \to 8$, regularized
by stochastic depth \citep{huang2016deep}. After each stage, a FiLM layer
\citep{perez2018film} applies a context-conditioned affine transformation
$\mathrm{FiLM}(x) = \gamma(\mathbf{c})\odot x + \beta(\mathbf{c})$
(initialized to identity), letting the sampled regime modulate feature
extraction at every scale. The final $8\times8$ feature map is then
flattened into 64 tokens --- augmented with a learned CLS token and a
projected context token --- and passed through a 3-layer Transformer encoder
\citep{vaswani2017attention}. This convolution-plus-attention hybrid, in the
spirit of bottleneck transformers \citep{srinivas2021botnet}, is deliberate:
a plain flatten-or-pool head would collapse the spatial layout precisely
where the task needs \emph{relational} reasoning over distant regions
(``the unexplored area lies beyond that wall, on the far side of the known
crash site''). The CLS output, concatenated with the mean of the spatial
tokens, is projected to a 1024-dimensional embedding.

\paragraph{Local encoder.}
The $13\times13$ patch --- cropped from the local stream at the coordinates
recovered from the Own\_Position channel --- is encoded by three
ConvNeXt blocks at constant width 96 with \emph{no} spatial downsampling
(one block dilated to widen the receptive field): on a grid where every cell
matters, each stride would destroy exactly the cell-level distinctions the
patch exists to preserve. A FiLM layer conditioned on the \emph{global
embedding} (rather than on the raw context) then modulates the local
features --- the strategic picture reshapes how tactical surroundings are
read --- before projection to a 384-dimensional embedding.

\paragraph{Dueling head.}
Global embedding, local embedding, and raw context are concatenated
($1024 + 384 + 5 = 1413$) and passed through a three-layer fully connected
trunk ($1536 \to 768 \to 384$, each with layer normalization and GELU) that
feeds the dueling decomposition of Section~\ref{subsec:bg-dqn}: separate
value and advantage heads recombined as
$Q = V + A - \operatorname{mean}(A)$.

\paragraph{Normalization discipline.}
The network uses layer normalization \citep{ba2016layer} and GRN
exclusively --- no batch normalization anywhere. The reason is operational:
batch statistics would make a batch-of-one greedy inference (as executed by
each drone at every turn) behave differently from a batched training
forward pass. With batch-size-independent normalization, training-time and
acting-time computations are identical by construction; the only module that
distinguishes the two modes is stochastic depth, which is inactive at
inference.

\subsection{Context vector}
\label{subsec:arch-context}

The context $\mathbf{c}_i \in [0,1]^5$ is the interface between domain
randomization and the network:
\[
\mathbf{c}_i \;=\;
\Bigl(
\tfrac{\rvis}{\rvis^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{\rcomm}{\rcomm^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{\nagents}{\nagents^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{i}{\nagents^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{\hat n_i}{\nagents^{\max}}
\Bigr),
\]
where the normalizers are the \emph{maxima of the randomization ranges},
derived programmatically from the configuration
(Section~\ref{subsec:arch-overview}). Each component has a distinct role:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \emph{Sensing and communication ranges} tell the policy which
    regime it is operating in, enabling regime-dependent behaviour
    (Section~\ref{subsec:bg-dr}) --- e.g.\ how much a corridor can be
    trusted as ``explored'' given the width of the vision window.
  \item \emph{Team size and agent identity.} The identity term is the
    symmetry breaker required by parameter sharing
    (Section~\ref{subsec:bg-marl}), and here it is not optional: all drones
    spawn on the \emph{same} cell with identical knowledge, so at the first
    round their observations coincide exactly. Without $i$ in the input, a
    shared deterministic policy would move all drones identically, forever.
    With it, the network can learn an implicit role assignment ---
    conditioned on identity, disperse in different directions.
  \item \emph{Believed alive count} $\hat n_i$ is the fault model's entry
    point into decision-making: as the believed team shrinks, the division
    of labour that made sense for $\nagents$ drones no longer does, and the
    policy can recalibrate --- e.g.\ widen its own effective sector.
\end{itemize}

Because $\hat n_i^t$ evolves within the episode
(Section~\ref{subsec:prob-fault}), the context is \emph{rebuilt at every
turn for every drone}; it is a per-decision input, not an episode constant.
Deliberately \emph{absent} from the context are the two remaining randomized
parameters: obstacle density (redundant --- the network sees actual
obstacles in its maps, a far richer signal than a scalar summary) and fault
probability (unknowable --- a real drone has no access to its own failure
rate, so training a dependence on it would build in an input that deployment
cannot supply, exactly the pitfall noted in Section~\ref{subsec:bg-dr}).

\subsection{Learning machinery}
\label{subsec:arch-learning}

\paragraph{Experience pipeline.}
Every drone's transitions flow through a per-drone $n$-step window into one
\emph{shared} replay buffer (uniform sampling, $2\times10^5$ capacity) ---
parameter sharing extends to experience: the network trains on the pooled
stream of the whole team. Per-drone windows are necessary because execution
is sequential: drone $i$'s ``next step'' is one round away, interleaved with
every other drone's turns, so windows must be threaded by agent rather than
by wall-clock order. Each emitted transition carries the pair
$(R^{(m)}, \gamma^m)$ --- the discounted $m$-step return and the bootstrap
discount, with $\gamma^m$ replaced by $0$ if the window hit a terminal
state --- so that termination-vs-truncation semantics
(Sections~\ref{subsec:bg-dqn} and~\ref{subsec:train-correctness}) are
decided \emph{once}, at emission time, and the update rule stays oblivious
to them. The window also implements the two fault-sensitive behaviours
motivated later: an immediate bootstrapped flush when its drone breaks, and
retroactive team crediting of the terminal bonus, which requires the
terminal round to still be pending (hence $n \geq 2$).

\paragraph{Update rule.}
Training performs standard Double-DQN regression
(Section~\ref{subsec:bg-dqn}) on sampled minibatches:
\[
y \;=\; R^{(m)} + \gamma^m \,
Q_{\theta^-}\!\bigl(s',\, \arg\max_{a'} Q_{\theta}(s', a')\bigr),
\]
minimizing the Huber loss under gradient-norm clipping, with a hard
synchronization of the target network every fixed number of gradient steps.
One gradient step runs every few agent-turns, so learning interleaves with
acting at a fixed ratio. (Hyperparameter values are consolidated in
Appendix~\ref{app:config}.)

\paragraph{Batched decentralized acting.}
At every round, action selection for the whole team costs at most one
forward pass: each live drone independently draws its exploration coin
($\varepsilon$-greedy), the greedy drones' inputs are stacked into one
batch, Q-values are computed jointly, and each drone's action mask is
applied to its own row before the $\arg\max$ (masked actions set to
$-\infty$; exploring drones sample uniformly among their legal actions).
This is an implementation convenience enabled by parameter sharing --- the
\emph{information} used for each drone's decision remains strictly its own
observation and context, preserving decentralized-execution semantics
(a point Section~\ref{subsec:mas-learning} returns to).

\subsection{Tooling and interaction modes}
\label{subsec:arch-tooling}

Beyond training, the same environment and agent serve four inspection and
evaluation workflows. An \emph{eval} mode renders greedy policy episodes in
a GUI; a \emph{play} mode hands drone 0 to the keyboard, which proved
valuable for sanity-checking reward mechanics and fault behaviour from the
inside; a \emph{simulate} mode exposes a configuration screen (team size,
vision, communication, obstacle density, and a fault-probability slider)
and runs batches of episodes with live rendering of maps, beliefs, and
per-drone status. The renderer is strictly read-only with respect to the
environment (Figure~\ref{fig:modules}).

For quantitative work, \texttt{eval\_checkpoints.py} ranks checkpoints on
identical fixed-seed instance sets --- the same seeds reproduce the same
layouts \emph{and} fault sequences (Section~\ref{subsec:arch-env}), making
scores comparable across checkpoints --- and
\texttt{testing/evaluate\_policy.py} runs the one-factor-at-a-time sweeps
used in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Both support an optional
\emph{auto-navigation} override: once a drone's maps contain the target, a
BFS planner on its \emph{known} map (unknown cells optimistically assumed
free, replanned every step) steers it there deterministically. This
override exists to decouple two abilities in analysis --- \emph{finding}
the target versus \emph{reaching} a found target --- and is confined to
evaluation tooling by an architectural invariant: it never runs during
training or during the in-training evaluation that selects checkpoints, as
that would corrupt both what is learned and how it is measured
(Section~\ref{subsec:train-correctness}).
