\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The system is implemented from scratch in Python. This section walks through its
architecture bottom-up: the codebase layout and its configuration-driven design,
the environment implementation, the tensor encoding of the observations of
Section~\ref{subsec:prob-obs}, the neural network that maps observations to
action values, the context vector that conditions it, the learning machinery
around it, and the interactive tooling used for inspection and evaluation.

The codebase separates four concerns (Figure~\ref{fig:modules}): the
\emph{environment} (\texttt{env/}) implements the world model of
Section~\ref{sec:problem}; the \emph{agent} (\texttt{agents/}) holds the network,
the replay and $n$-step buffers and the update rule; the \emph{training package}
(\texttt{training/}) owns the episode loop, curriculum staging and
domain-randomization sampling; and the \emph{GUI} (\texttt{gui/}) renders
episodes without ever influencing them. The entry points are thin wrappers over
these packages.

A single YAML file is the sole source of truth for every tunable quantity:
geometry and reward values, randomization ranges, curriculum phases, network and
optimizer hyperparameters, logging cadence. The consequence is methodological:
an experiment, including the full training curriculum, is specified by editing
configuration, not code. The same discipline governs derived quantities; the
normalizers of the context vector, for instance, are computed from the
randomization ranges rather than stated independently, so widening a range cannot
silently desynchronize the network's inputs.

\subsection{Environment implementation}
\label{subsec:arch-env}

\texttt{DroneSearchEnv} realizes the model of Section~\ref{subsec:prob-model}
behind a Gymnasium-style interface \citep{towers2024gymnasium}, with one
essential extension: the native stepping primitive is
\texttt{step\_agent(i, action)}, one call per drone per round in fixed
round-robin order, rather than a joint step (a standard \texttt{step(action)}
wrapper exists for API compliance, but the training loop drives
\texttt{step\_agent} directly). Episode setup follows a strict contract:
\texttt{set\_domain\_params(...)}, which installs
$\theta = (\rvis, \rcomm, \nagents, \rho, \pfault)$, then \texttt{reset()}, which
generates the world. Keeping the two separate matters because the same parameters
are also fed to the network as its context vector: the contract guarantees that
what the environment \emph{does} and what the network is \emph{told} can never
diverge.

\paragraph{World generation.}
\texttt{reset()} performs rejection sampling: it draws an obstacle map with
density $\rho$, forces the reference corner free, computes the BFS-reachable
region, and resamples (up to a bounded number of attempts) if that region is
trivial; the target is then drawn uniformly from the reachable free cells. All
randomness flows through a single seeded generator, and map generation consumes
it before the fault process does, so one seed reproduces the layout \emph{and}
the fault sequence, a property the evaluation protocol relies on.

\paragraph{Anatomy of a turn.}
A live drone's \texttt{step\_agent} call executes a fixed pipeline: (i)~the fault
coin flip (on a break, the rest of the pipeline is skipped and the call returns a
flagged no-op, the turn having been spent all the same); (ii)~move validation and
execution, accumulating the reward terms of Table~\ref{tab:reward};
(iii)~vision update of the drone's knowledge maps; (iv)~wreck-beacon detection;
(v)~communication fusion over the connectivity components; (vi)~update of the
team-level discovered set, from which the exploration bonus is computed;
(vii)~target check. A wreck's call skips everything but the clock. The action
mask is computed on demand per drone and returned alongside the observation, so
every consumer (training, evaluation, GUI) applies exactly the same legality
rule.

\subsection{Observation encoding}
\label{subsec:arch-obs}

The knowledge state $K_i^t$ reaches the network as two spatial tensors
(Table~\ref{tab:channels}), both full-map, normalized to $[0,1]$:

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{clll}
\toprule
\textbf{Ch.} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Stream} & \textbf{Encoding} \\
\midrule
0 & Visited        & both   & $\{0,1\}$: free cells discovered by drone $i$ (incl.\ fused) \\
1 & Obstacle       & both   & $\{0,1\}$: obstacles discovered by drone $i$ \\
2 & Trajectory     & both   & $\min(\tau_i(c)/5,\,1)$: own visit counts, saturating \\
3 & Target         & both   & $\{0,1\}$: target cell once seen (persistent memory) \\
4 & Own\_Position  & global & $\{0,1\}$: single $1$ at drone $i$'s cell \\
5 & Broken         & global & $\{0,1\}$: crash sites \emph{known to drone $i$} \\
4 & Other\_Position& local  & $\{0,1\}$: \emph{live} teammates currently within $V_i^t$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Observation channels. The \emph{global} stream
($6\times\gridsize\times\gridsize$) is consumed whole; the \emph{local}
stream ($5\times\gridsize\times\gridsize$) is cropped to a $13\times13$
patch centred on the drone before encoding. Channel 4 differs by stream: the
egocentric patch needs no own-position marker, but benefits from seeing
nearby teammates. Wrecks never appear as teammates: they surface in the
Broken channel instead, once known.}
\label{tab:channels}
\end{table}

Two encoding decisions deserve justification. First, \textbf{the drone's own
position is a spatial channel}, not an out-of-band coordinate pair: channel 4 of
the global stream holds a single $1$ at the drone's cell, so the convolutional
encoder can reason about position \emph{spatially}, in its relation to obstacles,
to the searched region and to known crash sites, while still recovering the
integer coordinates exactly ($\arg\max$ over that channel) whenever it needs
them, as the patch-cropping step does. Second, \textbf{fault knowledge is spatial
too}: the Broken channel marks the crash sites drone $i$ knows about, and only
those. Beyond the scalar belief $\hat n_i$, the \emph{location} of a crash tells
the network \emph{which region} lost its searcher, precisely what is needed to
re-cover a dead teammate's sector. The local stream then complements the global
one at a different scale: its $13\times13$ crop, sized to exceed the largest
vision window ($2\cdot6+1 = 13$), retains full resolution where cell-level
distinctions drive immediate action.

A single network (Figure~\ref{fig:network}) maps
$(o_i^{\mathrm{glob}}, o_i^{\mathrm{loc}}, \mathbf{c}_i)$, that is global stream,
local stream and context vector, to the four action values. It fuses three
information pathways: a \emph{global encoder} for strategic structure, a
\emph{local encoder} for tactical detail, and the context for regime awareness.

\paragraph{Global encoder.}
The global stream is processed by a ConvNeXt-style hierarchy
\citep{liu2022convnet}: three stages of inverted-bottleneck blocks (depthwise
$7\times7$ convolution, pointwise expansion, GELU, and Global Response
Normalization \citep{woo2023convnext}) with channel widths $96 \to 192 \to 384$
at spatial resolutions $32 \to 16 \to 8$, regularized by stochastic depth
\citep{huang2016deep}. After each stage a FiLM layer \citep{perez2018film}
applies a context-conditioned affine transformation
$\mathrm{FiLM}(x) = \gamma(\mathbf{c})\odot x + \beta(\mathbf{c})$ (initialized
to identity), letting the sampled regime modulate feature extraction at every
scale. The final $8\times8$ map is flattened into 64 tokens, augmented with a
learned CLS token and a projected context token, and passed through a 3-layer
Transformer encoder \citep{vaswani2017attention}, the CLS output being
concatenated with the mean of the spatial tokens and projected to a
1024-dimensional embedding. This convolution-plus-attention hybrid, in the spirit
of bottleneck transformers \citep{srinivas2021botnet}, is deliberate: a plain
flatten-or-pool head would collapse the spatial layout precisely where the task
needs \emph{relational} reasoning over distant regions (``the unexplored area
lies beyond that wall, on the far side of the known crash site'').

\paragraph{Local encoder.}
The $13\times13$ patch, cropped at the coordinates recovered from the
Own\_\allowbreak Position channel, is encoded by three ConvNeXt blocks at constant width
96 with \emph{no} spatial downsampling (one block dilated to widen the receptive
field): on a grid where every cell matters, a stride would destroy exactly the
cell-level distinctions the patch exists to preserve. A FiLM layer conditioned on
the \emph{global embedding} rather than on the raw context then modulates the
local features, so that the strategic picture reshapes how tactical surroundings
are read, before projection to a 384-dimensional embedding.

\paragraph{Dueling head.}
Global embedding, local embedding and raw context are concatenated
($1024 + 384 + 5 = 1413$) and passed through a three-layer fully connected trunk
($1536 \to 768 \to 384$, each with layer normalization and GELU) that feeds the
dueling decomposition of Section~\ref{subsec:bg-dqn}: separate value and
advantage heads recombined as $Q = V + A - \operatorname{mean}(A)$.

\paragraph{Normalization discipline.}
The network uses layer normalization \citep{ba2016layer} and GRN exclusively, no
batch normalization anywhere, for an operational reason: batch statistics would
make the batch-of-one greedy inference each drone executes at every turn behave
differently from a batched training forward pass. With batch-size-independent
normalization the two are identical by construction; the only module that
distinguishes them is stochastic depth, inactive at inference.

\subsection{Context vector}
\label{subsec:arch-context}

The context $\mathbf{c}_i \in [0,1]^5$ is the interface between domain
randomization and the network:
\[
\mathbf{c}_i \;=\;
\Bigl(
\tfrac{\rvis}{\rvis^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{\rcomm}{\rcomm^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{\nagents}{\nagents^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{i}{\nagents^{\max}},\;
\tfrac{\hat n_i}{\nagents^{\max}}
\Bigr),
\]
where the normalizers are the \emph{maxima of the randomization ranges}, derived
programmatically from the configuration. Each component has a distinct role:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \emph{Sensing and communication ranges} tell the policy which regime it
    is operating in, enabling regime-dependent behaviour: how much a corridor
    can be trusted as ``explored'' given the width of the vision window, for
    instance.
  \item \emph{Team size and agent identity.} The identity term is the symmetry
    breaker required by parameter sharing (Section~\ref{subsec:bg-marl}), and
    here it is not optional: all drones spawn on the \emph{same} cell with
    identical knowledge, so without $i$ in the input a shared deterministic
    policy would move them identically, forever. With it, the network can learn
    an implicit role assignment: conditioned on identity, disperse in different
    directions.
  \item \emph{Believed alive count} $\hat n_i$ is the fault model's entry point
    into decision-making. As the believed team shrinks, the division of labour
    that made sense for $\nagents$ drones no longer does, and the policy can
    recalibrate, widening its own effective sector.
\end{itemize}

Because $\hat n_i^t$ evolves within the episode, the context is \emph{rebuilt at
every turn for every drone}; it is a per-decision input, not an episode constant.
Deliberately \emph{absent} from the context are the two remaining randomized
parameters: obstacle density, which is redundant since the network sees actual
obstacles in its maps, a far richer signal than a scalar summary; and fault
probability, which is unknowable, since a real drone has no access to its own
failure rate, so training a dependence on it would build in an input that
deployment cannot supply, exactly the pitfall noted in
Section~\ref{subsec:bg-dr}.

\subsection{Learning machinery}
\label{subsec:arch-learning}

\paragraph{Experience pipeline.}
Every drone's transitions flow through a per-drone $n$-step window into one
\emph{shared} replay buffer (uniform sampling, $2\times10^5$ capacity):
parameter sharing extends to experience, and the network trains on the pooled
stream of the whole team. Per-drone windows are necessary because execution is
sequential: drone $i$'s ``next step'' is one round away, interleaved with every
other drone's turns, so windows must be threaded by agent rather than by
wall-clock order. Each emitted transition carries the pair
$(R^{(m)}, \gamma^m)$, the discounted $m$-step return and the bootstrap discount,
with $\gamma^m$ replaced by $0$ if the window hit a terminal state; so
termination-vs-truncation semantics are decided \emph{once}, at emission time,
and the update rule stays oblivious to them. The window also implements the two
fault-sensitive behaviours motivated in Section~\ref{subsec:train-correctness}:
an immediate bootstrapped flush when its drone breaks, and retroactive team
crediting of the terminal bonus, which requires the terminal round to still be
pending (hence $n \geq 2$).

\paragraph{Update rule.}
Training is standard Double-DQN regression on the target of
Section~\ref{subsec:bg-dqn}, computed from the stored $(R^{(m)}, \gamma^m)$ pair,
minimizing the Huber loss under gradient-norm clipping with hard target
synchronization; one gradient step runs every few agent-turns, so learning
interleaves with acting at a fixed ratio. Hyperparameter values are consolidated
in Appendix~\ref{app:config}.

\paragraph{Batched decentralized acting.}
At every round, action selection for the whole team costs at most one forward
pass: each live drone independently draws its exploration coin
($\varepsilon$-greedy), the greedy drones' inputs are stacked into one batch,
Q-values are computed jointly, and each drone's action mask is applied to its own
row before the $\arg\max$ (masked actions set to $-\infty$; exploring drones
sample uniformly among their legal actions). This is an implementation
convenience enabled by parameter sharing: the \emph{information} used for each
drone's decision remains strictly its own observation and context, preserving
decentralized-execution semantics.

\subsection{Tooling and interaction modes}
\label{subsec:arch-tooling}

Beyond training, the same environment and agent serve three interactive
workflows: an \emph{eval} mode that renders greedy policy episodes; a
\emph{play} mode that hands drone 0 to the keyboard, valuable for
sanity-checking reward mechanics and fault behaviour from the inside; and a
\emph{simulate} mode with a configuration screen (team size, vision,
communication, density, and a fault-probability slider) that runs batches of
episodes with live rendering of maps, beliefs and per-drone status
(Figure~\ref{fig:gui}). The renderer is strictly read-only with respect to the
environment (Figure~\ref{fig:modules}).

For quantitative work, \texttt{eval\_checkpoints.py} ranks checkpoints on
identical fixed-seed instance sets, the same seeds reproducing the same layouts
\emph{and} fault sequences, while \texttt{testing/\allowbreak evaluate\_policy.py}
runs the
one-factor-at-a-time sweeps of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Both support an
optional \emph{auto-navigation} override: once a drone's maps contain the target,
a BFS planner on its \emph{known} map (unknown cells optimistically assumed free,
replanned every step) steers it there deterministically. The override exists to
decouple two abilities in analysis, \emph{finding} the target versus
\emph{reaching} a found target, and is confined to evaluation tooling by an
architectural invariant: it never runs during training, nor during the
in-training evaluation that selects checkpoints. In the training loop it would
corrupt the data distribution, since transitions would reflect the planner's
policy rather than the network's; in checkpoint selection it would corrupt the
metric, checkpoints being ranked partly on the planner's competence.
